Bell test and CHSH inequality simulator

A Bell test asks whether measurement results on two distant particles can be explained by pre-existing local hidden values. Quantum mechanics says no, and the CHSH inequality is the standard way to check.

Open the bell / chsh simulator

The CHSH quantity

Two observers each pick between two measurement angles (a, a′ and b, b′) and record correlations E. The combination S = E(a,b) − E(a,b′) + E(a′,b) + E(a′,b′) is bounded by |S| ≤ 2 for any local hidden-variable model.

The quantum result

For polarization-entangled photons in the state (|HH⟩ + |VV⟩)/√2, the correlation is E(a,b) = cos 2(a − b). With angles 0°, 45° for one side and 22.5°, 67.5° for the other, S reaches 2√2 ≈ 2.83, the Tsirelson bound. That clearly exceeds the classical limit of 2.

Why it matters

Experiments of this type, awarded the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics, rule out local hidden-variable explanations and underpin quantum key distribution and quantum networks.
